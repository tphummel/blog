When I was in eighth grade and we studied us history, we did a unit on us states. Students each chose states and did a report and presentation on each state. I was nervous about public speaking but I learned the states would present in the order they were admitted to the union. Which would make Delaware the first presentation. This seemed appealing to me to be first to go and get it done with. I dreaded the idea of waiting around for days watching presentation after presentation knowing mine was still yet to be done. I volunteered for Delaware when others were angling to get Hawaii, Alaska, Arizona, and New Mexico. I remember the feeling of sitting down after a presentation I was proud of, knowing I was done and could watch the rest of the presentations without feeling anxious.

Later, as a track and field athlete, it was common to feel nerves before a race. If the gun would just go off right now i could get running and I would calm down. It was the waiting to begin which can drive you crazy. A 7am race was great. Having your only race of the day be the final event of a meet was stressful. Hearing the gun go off over and over, seeing your teammates compete while you are still fresh, results in small, repeated adrenaline dumps which can drain you.[^1]

As a professional if I've got 15 meetings to do over a week, and some will be more difficult than others, stack the hardest ones first, prepare for them thoroughly, and get them done well. Then get to the moderate and lowest risk last.

Get after it. Don't delay. Get to work. Choose Delaware.

[^1]: unrelated, but my favorite format was racing four (or more?!) times in a single meet and hobbling onto the bus afterward, exhausted and content.
