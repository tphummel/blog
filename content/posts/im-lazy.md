In the 2010s software engineers used to brag about being lazy. They would say things like, “I’m lazy so I built this automation so I didn’t have to do X anymore”. I found it curious at the time when I would hear statements like this. The speaker’s emphasis would usually be on the automation itself but I couldn’t help but fixate on the motivation. 

for myself in my career I had an inflection point when I realized there was no shortcut to building software that mattered. It took a relentless pursuit of solving a problem, learning what I didn’t know yet to achieve a goal, and building deep expertise in an area. 

So hearing laziness presented as a virtue was confusing to me. I never pressed it at the time, I figured maybe there were smarter people who could get great outcomes without taking my exact path. 

In 2026 our profession is changing significantly with agentic programming upending how software is built. Snake oil salespeople will tell you the result will be 10 hour work weeks with agents taking care of the rest. Of course that isn’t true. 

We’re all working hard to keep up, reading what experts are having success with, testing out a new model, tuning the newest harness, writing a new skill or plugin. Then once this new way of building is “solved” and everyone achieves a new level of leveraged productivity, there will be so much more value to create that we’ll be working more than ever. I find this energizing. 

Imagine a teammate coming to a meeting in 2026 saying, “I’m lazy so I had Anthropic do my work for me.”
